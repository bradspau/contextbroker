A link between two interfaces is a stable node. What the network held about it at a given time is
carried by link fluents, each with a valid interval and a knowledge interval. This note describes how
to check what the topology said, and when it said it.

Start with the two instants of the question: the valid time, which is when the fact held in the world,
and the knowledge time, which is when the broker held the record. A link fluent is in force when the
valid time falls inside its valid interval and the knowledge time falls inside its knowledge interval.
Without a knowledge time, a record that has not been superseded is the one in force.

Read the neighbours of the device or interface at the time of interest, then read them again at a
second time before or after the suspected change. A neighbour that appears or disappears between the
two readings marks a change in the recorded topology. It does not say why the link changed or whether
the network agrees.

An interface that is in the graph with no link in force then has no neighbours, which is a real
answer. An interface that is not in the graph at all is missing, which is a different answer.

If two link fluents are in force on one link, or a record names other than two usable interfaces,
report the conflict and return both records. The recorded topology is configured state. A reported
event that disagrees with it is information to show next to it, and neither side is ranked.
